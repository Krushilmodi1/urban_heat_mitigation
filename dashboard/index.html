Executive Research Summary

This research formulates an urban heat island mitigation framework for the semi-arid metropolitan region of Ahmedabad, Gujarat. Using a 1 km × 1 km spatial discretization, the project integrates physical thermodynamic conservation laws into deep neural networks to project surface skin cooling (ΔLST) and near-surface air cooling (ΔT_air).

Key Analytical Takeaways:
  • Spatial Generalization: Evaluating on contiguous 8 km blocks reduces spatial autocorrelation leakage and provides a rigorous out-of-sample evaluation, revealing that tree ensembles overfit local coordinates (R² drops from 0.42 to 0.03).
  • PINN Regularization: The physics loss (λ_pde = 0.1) acts as a physical regularizer, cutting PDE energy residual by 22.2% (from 0.962 down to 0.748) while maintaining 0.32 °C MAE.
  • Canopy Decoupling: Pedestrian 2m air cooling is attenuated relative to radiometric rooftop skin cooling (ΔT_air ≈ 0.32 × ΔLST).
  • Spatial Prioritization: Multi-Criteria UHVI balances thermal hazard (40%), structural footprint (40%), and vegetative deficit (20%) using empirical rank percentiles.

Physics-Informed Regularization Deep-Dive

Energy Conservation PDE Residual Loss:

The PINN optimizes a composite loss balancing empirical observation with thermodynamic conservation laws:

L_total = L_data + λ_pde · L_pde   (λ_pde = 0.1)

The steady-state 2D thermal conduction-advection-source differential operator penalizes non-physical gradient spikes across space:

f_pde = u·(∂T/∂x) + v·(∂T/∂y) - α·∇²T - S(x, y) / (ρ · c_p · h)

Why the PINN has lower R² (0.014 vs 0.056 for Ridge): The physics loss acts strictly as a regularizer. It penalizes non-physical gradient spikes across unobserved spatial regimes, slightly reducing statistical curve-fitting flexibility while driving the thermal PDE residual MSE down from 0.962 to 0.748 (22.2% reduction).

Note: A 22.2% reduction in the implemented PDE residual reflects numerical regularization under the formulated 2D steady-state heat equation; it does not demonstrate real-world physical validity in the absence of empirical satellite ground-truth observations.

Residual Spatial Autocorrelation (Moran's I):

-0.006 (p = 0.44)

A residual Moran's I = -0.006 (p = 0.44) fails to reject the null hypothesis of spatial randomness under inverse-distance weighting at this scale. While this demonstrates that the contiguous 8 km spatial block holdout successfully avoided naive spatial autocorrelation leakage, it does not constitute proof of absolute spatial independence across all spatial processes or scales.

Synthetic Target Variation & Modest Test R² Context:

Total target variation across the 780 spatial holdout cells is narrow (standard deviation σ = 0.407 °C, Var(LST) = 0.166 °C²). In the offline test generator (tests/synthetic_data.py), an independent Gaussian perturbation with nominal σ = 0.35 °C was added to cell temperatures. Combined with the low prototype building coverage (mean 0.08%) and the strict 8 km spatial block split evaluating models on unseen geographic regimes, this high noise-to-signal ratio limits explainable out-of-sample variance across all model architectures (test R² = 0.01 – 0.06), while absolute errors remain small (RMSE ≈ 0.40 °C, MAE ≈ 0.32 °C).

Mathematical Formulations

1. Urban Heat Vulnerability Index (UHVI):

UHVI_i = 0.40·RankPct(LST_i) + 0.40·RankPct(ρ_bld,i) + 0.20·RankPct(1 - NDVI_i)

Empirical rank percentiles (empirical CDF ranks scaled to [0, 1]) are computed across all cells to prevent extreme right-skewed building density outliers from distorting vulnerability prioritization.

2. Non-Linear Cooling Saturation:

ΔLST_comb = 6.5 · [1 - exp(-ΣΔLST_i / (6.5 · 0.85))]

3. Air Temperature Coupling:

ΔT_air ≈ 0.32 · ΔLST (Canopy boundary layer)

Known Research Limitations
  • Earth Engine Authentication: Live USGS Landsat rasters require interactive browser OAuth (earthengine authenticate). In offline mode, the pipeline operates on the calibrated synthetic proxy raster.
  • Building Sample Extent: D:\395_buildings.csv contains 4,777 buildings inside the study area, resulting in 0.08% mean density. Real city-scale applications require uncompressed municipal building datasets.
  • Physics Regularization vs. Ground Truth: The 22.2% reduction in the implemented PDE residual indicates compliance with the formulated 2D heat energy operator; it does not substitute for empirical satellite ground-truth validation.
  • Synthetic Counterfactuals: Modeled cooling values represent parameter sensitivity projections under the prototype data-generating process, not empirically measured Ahmedabad microclimate cooling.
  • Microscale CFD: The 1 km grid resolution averages building canyon geometry. Localized street-level turbulence requires microscale modeling (ENVI-met).

Urban Heat Island (UHI) Challenge in Ahmedabad
Ahmedabad faces extreme summer heat hazards exceeding 45 °C ambient temperatures. Rapid urbanization and dense built-up surfaces amplify radiometric Land Surface Temperatures (LST).

Core Research Question: Can physics-informed deep learning predict localized urban cooling potentials while respecting fundamental thermodynamic energy conservation laws, and how does it compare to empirical statistical baselines under strict spatial holdout validation?

Slide 3 of 5 | Spatial Machine Learning
Mitigating Spatial Autocorrelation Leakage
Traditional random k-fold cross-validation suffers from severe spatial autocorrelation leakage (Tobler's First Law), creating inflated, misleading R² scores.

To mitigate spatial leakage and evaluate out-of-sample geographic generalization, we partitioned the domain into contiguous 8.0 km spatial blocks, holding out 780 cells (23.5% of the domain across 10 held-out blocks vs. 28 training blocks). While zero coordinate overlap prevents direct sample re-use, spatial autocorrelation across block boundaries can still persist.

Key Findings:
  • Random Forest collapsed from train R² = 0.42 to test R² = 0.03, demonstrating that tree models heavily memorize spatial coordinates rather than generalizing across held-out geographic regimes.
  • Total target variation is narrow (σ = 0.41 °C) and contains injected synthetic Gaussian perturbation (nominal σ = 0.35 °C). Combined with sparse prototype building footprints (mean 0.08%) and strict 8 km geographic block evaluation, this high noise-to-signal ratio limits explainable out-of-sample R² scores (0.01 – 0.06) across all model families, while maintaining small absolute errors (RMSE ≈ 0.40 °C, MAE ≈ 0.32 °C).
  • Residual Moran's I = -0.006 (p = 0.44) does not detect significant residual spatial autocorrelation under inverse-distance weighting at this scale; however, failing to reject spatial randomness does not mathematically prove independence across all spatial processes or scales.
Slide 4 of 5 | PINN Physics Constraint
The Role of the Physics Regularization Loss
The Physics-Informed Neural Network incorporates the steady-state 2D thermal conduction-advection PDE into its loss function (λ_pde = 0.1).

Does the PINN outperform Ridge in statistical R²? No. Ridge Regression achieves the best statistical holdout fit with test R² = 0.056 (RMSE 0.396 °C), while the PINN achieves test R² = 0.014 (RMSE 0.405 °C), trading minor statistical fit for derivative constraint.

What is the actual contribution of the physics loss? The PDE energy loss acts as a derivative regularizer. It reduced thermal PDE residual MSE by 22.2% (from 0.962 down to 0.748), penalizing unphysical spatial fluctuations without degrading holdout test error (MAE 0.32 °C). The 22.2% reduction in the implemented PDE residual reflects regularization under the simplified 2D energy equation, but does not establish real-world physical validity in the absence of measured satellite ground truth.
